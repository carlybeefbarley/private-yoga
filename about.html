The person behind the practice

A little more about me

Photo coming soon

I didn't fall in love with yoga the first time I tried it. My first experience was a college PE class that I ended up dropping. It would be another twelve years before I found my way back.

When I did, I wasn't looking for anything particularly profound. I was going through a difficult time, happened to live above a yoga studio, and figured I'd give their free trial a shot. I noticed almost immediately that I felt calmer and a little less caught up in my own head. Before long, I was practicing nearly every day.

What started as a way to feel better became something much bigger.

Yoga gave me a space to pay attention to myself in a way I hadn't before. I began noticing patterns in my thoughts, emotions, and behavior, and learning that I could experience a feeling without being completely consumed by it.

Over time, the philosophy of yoga began to find its way into my life, sometimes before I even had the language for it. Two of the niyamas that resonate with me are tapas (discipline) and svadhyaya (self-study). I love that I can return to the same practice day after day and have a completely different experience. Sometimes I feel strong and focused; other days I'm restless and distracted. The practice is showing up anyway, noticing what's there, and getting curious about it.

That curiosity has followed me well beyond my mat. I'm unique, like everyone else, and figuring out what makes me me is a lifelong project. There's always another layer to peel back, another perspective to consider, another opportunity to respond with more intention.

Finding my way into teaching

Becoming a yoga teacher wasn't originally part of the plan. I signed up for my first 200-hour teacher training simply because I wanted to immerse myself more deeply in a practice I'd come to love. Over time, my curiosity grew into a desire to share what I was learning, and I eventually completed a second training and began teaching.

During COVID, I moved from Seattle back to my hometown of Rochester, New York, where the yoga community was much smaller. I missed the creative, engaging classes I'd grown to love, so I started offering the kind of practice I wanted to see: thoughtful sequencing, playful transitions, and plenty of room to explore.

Since then, I've taught in studios, gyms, schools, and other spaces in Rochester, New York City, and Seattle. My teaching has evolved alongside my own practice, weaving more of the philosophy and mindfulness that make yoga so meaningful to me into the physical experience.

How I teach today

I think good teaching starts with paying attention.

I can plan a class down to the last detail, but if the people in front of me need something different that day, the plan should be able to change. Bodies are different. Energy changes. What feels wonderful one day might feel completely wrong the next.

That's a big part of what draws me to private yoga. Having the time and space to get to know someone and build a practice around their individual needs feels like a natural extension of everything I've learned through yoga.

I still love a strong, playful flow, a great playlist, and the occasional challenge that surprises you. But I also believe there's value in slowing down, asking questions, and learning to listen to yourself.

To me, yoga isn't about becoming endlessly calm or perfectly balanced. It's an ongoing practice of paying attention, showing up, and moving through life with a little more awareness and intention.

And there's always more to learn.
